Adhere strictly to these editing guidelines and pet peeves:

### 1. Grammar, Spelling, & Style Nitpicks:
*   "Oh well" must be corrected to "Oh, well"
*   "Goodluck" must be corrected to "Good luck"
*   "Nevermind" must be corrected to "Never mind"
*   "alright" must be corrected to "all right"
*   "Yeah yeah" must be corrected to "Yeah, yeah"
*   Never use "till"; always use "'til"
*   Change elongated variations like "Ohh" to "Oh"
*   Even if the official lyric video contains spelling errors or lacks diacritics, you must correct them. Do not blindly follow official source errors.

### 2. Line-Ending Punctuation:
*   No punctuation is allowed at the end of any lyric line except for a question mark (?) or quotation marks ("). Strip away all trailing commas, periods, or exclamation marks.

### 3. Deities & Native Languages (Diacritics):
*   Always respect and apply reverential capitalization for deities.
*   Respect native languages and utilize proper diacritics or local scripts. For example, in Yoruba:
    *   "Oya" must be written as "Ó yá" (for non-deities).
    *   "gbedu" must be written as "gbẹ̀du" (unless it is part of an artist's official name which does not use diacritics).

### 4. Genius Formatting & HTML Tags:
*   Genius annotation brackets are formatted as: `[lyrics](3828192)`
*   HTML formatting (such as `<i>` or `<b>`) is reserved strictly for separate/secondary vocals. Do not use HTML tags for same-artist ad-libs or vocals.
*   If formatting ad-libs for separate vocals, place them inside parentheses within HTML tags, like so: `(<i>adlib</i>)`
*   Even when an ad-lib or vocal is enclosed in HTML tags, always capitalize the very first word.
Note: You must repect official lyrics as it's from the artists themselves. We don't know more than the artist that wrote and sung the lyrics
Begin your response with your harsh rating and critique, followed by the corrected, bolded lyrics.

<h1>General Formatting</h1>

[✧](15050527) This section covers general formatting rules.

<b>Numbering</b>

[Verses are the only tag that should be enumerated,](16107272) and are labeled in ascending order: [Verse 1], [Verse 2], [Verse 3], etc. If the song only contains a single verse, do not enumerate, even if the single verse is repeated.

<b>Artist Identifiers in Headers</b>

This section will explain when to add [artist identifiers](15057423) to headers and break down the process to correctly add these artist identifiers.

<ul>
<li>[If a song has only one artist on the track it does not need an artist identifier added unless it is a solo track on a group project.](15057547)</li>
<li>[We can support adding up to 4 artist combinations to a section in the header using this process.](15057652)</li>
<li>[Do <b>not</b> add formatting if more than 4 artist combinations are in a section.](15095655)</li>
<li>For samples and producer tags, include the vocalist if known.</li>
<li>For movie samples, the actor/actress' name is used instead of the character name.</li>
</ul>

<b>[-> How to add each type of formatting](15056015)</b>

<h1>Verse</h1>

[✧](15050615) Similar to how paragraphs are the body of a letter or essay, the Verse is like a stanza in poetry. The length is usually larger than the rest of the components. Though not all Verses have a rhyme scheme, it is characteristic for Verses to follow a 'AABB' or 'ABAB' format. [-> Click here for examples.](16286549)

<h1>Chorus/Refrain/Hook</h1>

[✧](15050634)

<b>Chorus</b>
An independent section which is usually repeated, with identical harmonic and melodic structure. The lyrics of the Chorus often contain the song's lyrical hook, which serves to make the tune more recognizable and, in many cases, coerce the audience to sing along. [-> Click here for examples.](15050804)

<b>Refrain</b>
A Refrain is a line or set of lines that repeat at the end of each Verse without any change rhythmically, harmonically, or melodically. Usually a refrain is 1 to 2 lines maximum. [-> Click here for examples.](15057491)

Note: The refrain can often be confused with the Chorus. A Refrain is a repeated section of the verse. A Chorus (or a Pre-Chorus) will have a distinct change in "feel" that separates itself from the Verse. In fact, many songs with a refrain do NOT have a chorus; of course, there are always exceptions.

[<b>Hook</b>](34151858)
<i><b>Genius no longer uses the [Hook] tag. If a section is labeled as [Hook], edit the lyrics or submit a lyric proposal to change it to [Chorus] or [Refrain].</b></i>

<h1>Bridge/Breakdown</h1>

[✧](15050640)

<b>Bridge</b>
A bridge is the point in song when it completely changes, sounding melodically and/or lyrically different before it transitions back into original form. It can appear anywhere and rarely appears more than once.  [-> Click here for examples.](15059317)

<b>Breakdown</b>
Often used in [Metalcore](https://genius.com/tags/metalcore), [Metal](https://genius.com/tags/metal), [Thrash Metal](https://genius.com/tags/thrash-metal), [Post-Hardcore](https://genius.com/tags/post-hardcore), [Punk-Rock](https://genius.com/tags/punk-rock), [Nu-Metal](https://genius.com/tags/nu-metal) and other genres of the same family, a Breakdown is a section in which the melody changes abruptly. This change is often composed of simple but heavy guitar and bass riffs, pounding drums, and heavy screaming. This is the part of the song where headbanging is most prominent. [-> Click here for examples.](15041510)

<h1>Intro/Outro/Skit</h1>

[✧](15050594)

<b>Intro</b>
An Intro always appears at the beginning of the song or Part, though not all songs will include an Intro. They are often instrumentals with no words but sometimes there are people talking or buildup involved. Depending on the genre, there are many variations. If there's no lyrics and/or dialogue present, an [Instrumental Intro] header can be used when it's more than around 1 minute long, otherwise leave blank. [-> Click here for examples.](15042122)

<b>Outro</b>
An Outro always appears at the end of the song or Part, though not all songs will include an Outro. Lyrically, the section is represented by either repeating a line as the track fades out or repeating a section of a Chorus or Verse, sometimes with a slight change in the lyrics to indicate a resolution to the song's climax. Outros can consist of seemingly-random dialogue. If there's no lyrics and/or dialogue present, an [Instrumental Outro] header can be used when it's more than around 1 minute long, otherwise leave blank. [-> Click here for examples.](15059750)

<b>Skit</b>
While mostly occurring as their own full song, sometimes songs have a Skit in them—not to be confused with the Intro, Outro, or Interlude. If there is action in a part of the song that is separated from the beat, then it is a Skit. The key to this is action. This means something has to be happening that appears to be separate from the song. If the artist stops rapping and cuts the beat just to talk, then it is not a Skit because there is no action. [-> Click here for examples.](15041469)

<h1>Pre-Chorus/Post-Chorus</h1>

[✧](15050624)

<b>Pre-Chorus</b>
An often-repeated, semi-independent section that appears between the Verse and Chorus. The Pre-Chorus differs both musically and lyrically from both the Verse and the Chorus, but always appears in conjunction with the Chorus. This is also colloquially referred to as the "build" or "climb." [-> Click here for examples.](16286666)

<b>Post-Chorus</b>
A semi-independent section that works inversely to the Pre-Chorus, serving to link the Chorus to a succeeding Verse. A Post-Chorus differs both musically and lyrically from the Verse, Pre-Chorus, and Chorus, but always appears in conjunction with the Chorus. On occasion, a Post-Chorus will also double as a lyrical and/or musical Outro. [-> Click here for examples.](16309411)

<h1>Interlude/Segue</h1>

[✧](15050645)

<b>Interlude</b>
An Interlude is an instrumental or lyrical section which introduces a different feel than any other previous part of song. They are used to connect different sections of a song that does not follow a standard song structure. This is not to be mistaken with a Segue which connects two different songs within one track. [-> Click here for examples.](16309545)

<b>Segues & Parts</b>
A Segue is a musical, lyrical, or spoken passage that is used to connect two distinct entities—sometimes whole songs—within a single track using a non-standard structure. <b>Only label </b><b>the</b><b> Segue if there is dialogue connecting two sections together.</b>

If a song contains multiple sections, label each section with a "Part" header. [Part headers are accompanied by Roman numerals in ascending order, similar to Verses. If the Parts of a song are named, include a colon followed by the name of the Part.](16309499) If a song contains multiple sections, continue to number the Verses in order as if they were one continuous song. [-> Click here for examples.](15041887)

<h1>Instrumentation</h1>

[✧](15050650)

<b>Instrumental</b>
Instrumentals on the other hand are when groups play without vocalists and either coordinate in symphonies or jam together.

<ul>

[-> Click here for examples.](15041690)

<b>Instrumental Bre</b><b>ak</b>
Breaks involve instruments or percussion being played between different sections to help create variety in the arrangement or build up to the next Verse or Chorus.

<b>Solos</b>
Solos are Instrumental sections where a single performer shows off an improvised or written, extended melody, showcasing their talent. More often than not, a specific instrument rises above the others, hence there could be a [Guitar Solo], [Drum Solo], or [Saxophone Solo]. [-> Click here for examples.](15041652)

Note: Some solos/instrumental breaks have vocals during the solo. These can be transcribed under these headers. [-> Click here for examples.](36931432)

<h3>What is a Transcriber?</h3>

A Genius Transcriber is a trusted contributor who has proven they can consistently contribute high quality transcriptions. It's also important that they exercise sound judgment when adding or editing lyrics, accepting or rejecting other users' lyric edit proposals, and marking a song's lyrics as complete. Each song that a Transcriber transcribes or edits should be as accurate and complete as possible and match up with Genius' established formatting and style guidelines.

Transcribers are leaders on the site, setting a high bar not only for lyrical quality but also through interactions with other community members. Transcribers are expected to uphold and adhere to Genius transcription etiquette for new releases and work out lyrical discrepancies amicably with other users when debates arise.

<h3>How do I become a Transcriber?</h3>

The best way to earn the role is to sync up with a mentor who can review your transcriptions, give you feedback on how you can improve, and help determine your eligibility for the role. Moderators and Staff can grant the Transcriber role, but anyone with transcription experience on Genius can help mentor you!

If you feel your contributions adhere to our transcription guidelines, tag @mentors in the comments section of songs you've transcribed and a member of our mentorship team will take a look. You can also reach out to a Moderator directly who can either help you out or point you in the right direction. A great way to get noticed is to actively participate in our [transcribers forum](http://genius.com/transcribers) and/or [Discord channel.](https://genius.com/discussions/408236-Welcome-to-the-genius-community-discord-server)

<b>Before reaching out, ask yourself a few things:</b>

<ul>
<li>Do I frequently transcribe songs and edit lyrics / submit lyric edit proposals on songs transcribed by others?</li>
<li>Do my transcriptions and lyric edits consistently adhere to Genius' formatting standards?</li>
<li>Am I confident in which lyrics I'm sure of and which lyrics I need help with?</li>
<li>Am I aware of Genius' [transcription etiquette](https://genius.com/9290590) and adhere to it?</li>
<li>Do my metadata edits only reflect official sources and not speculated credits?</li>

<h3>Can I lose my role as a Transcriber?</h3>

Yes. As a Transcriber, your transcriptions, lyric edits, and songs you mark as complete will be scrutinized. Transcriptions should have strong lyrical accuracy, be formatted correctly, and follow Genius' grammatical conventions. These standards apply to both transcriptions you complete as well as transcriptions you approve. If the quality of your transcriptions is consistently subpar or you abuse the role in any way, you may lose the Transcriber role. However, if you demonstrate consistent improvement in transcription quality, you may be able to regain the Transcriber role.

<b>Here are the guidelines for losing the Transcriber role:</b>

If your transcriptions and lyric edits fall below standard, a mentor will reach out to give you feedback and help you get back on track. Making less than 50 transcriptions or transcription approvals in the time span of a year will also prompt mentors to review your transcriptions.

In either case, if your transcriptions and lyric edits are consistently below standard or you show an unwillingness to improve, you may lose your role.

If you are found to early claim a song page, you will be issued a role-removal warning. If you early claim again, you will lose your Transcriber role.

In the event that you lose the Transcriber role, you may attempt to earn it back by continuing to practice with your mentor(s) until you're consistently contributing great transcriptions and lyric edits again.

On Genius, we have a zero-tolerance policy for abusive or exploitative behavior. If you are abusing/harassing other users, breaking forum guidelines, exploiting the IQ system, or plagiarizing, you will lose your Transcriber role.

To stay up to date with best practices and our evolving transcription standards, follow the [Transcribers Forum](https://genius.com/transcribers) and [join us on Discord.](https://genius.com/discussions/408236-Welcome-to-the-genius-community-discord-server)

<h3>What is an Editor?</h3>

A Genius Editor is a contributor who has proven that they can consistently contribute high-quality annotations, song bios, album bios, and artist bios. They must also exercise sound judgment when accepting or rejecting annotations, suggestions, comments, and Q+A. Each annotation an editor creates, edits, or accepts should add knowledge to the referent and be written with style, without formatting, grammar, spelling, or punctuation errors.

Editors are leaders on the site, setting a high bar for quality through annotations and interactions with other community members. This includes providing feedback to other contributors, participating in forum discussions maturely and constructively, helping mentor new users, and encouraging great content.

<h3>How do I become an Editor?</h3>

If you feel your contributions adhere to the Contributor Guidelines, tag [<b>@mentors</b>](https://genius.com/mentors) in the suggestion section, and a member of our editorial community will take a look. Alternatively, you can send a message to a <b>[community staff member](https://genius.com/Genius-moderator-and-staffer-contact-listing-annotated)</b>, and they will help guide you through the process. A great way to get noticed is to take the <b>weekly </b><b>Annotation</b><b> Survey</b> that's pinned to the [music forum.](https://genius.com/music)

<b>Before reaching out, ask yourself a few things:</b>

<ul>
<li>Do my annotations highlight all the relevant lyrics?</li>
<li>Do my annotations teach me something about the lyrics, song, and/or artist?</li>
<li>Are the facts in my annotations credible and well-sourced?</li>
<li>Do the pictures, links, and/or videos add depth to this annotation?</li>
<li>Are my annotations written in complete sentences with correct grammar and punctuation?</li>
</ul>

If the answer to these questions is yes, chances are you should be an Editor!

<h3>Can I lose my role as an Editor?</h3>

Yes. As an Editor, your annotations and edits will be scrutinized. You may lose your role if you accept poor-quality annotations without editing or make sloppy annotations. However, if your edits, annotations, and judgement improve when accepting and rejecting annotations, you can become an Editor again.

<b>Here are the guidelines for losing the Editor role:</b>

<ul>
<li>If you've been an Editor for under six months and your editorial output is not up to par with our editorial standards, you will be given two warnings. You will lose your role if your output continues to be below standard.</li>
<li>If you've been an Editor for more than six months and your editorial output is not up to par with our editorial standards, you will lose your role on the spot. Contributors who have been Editors for over six months should know editorial standards well.</li>
<li>If you are abusing/harassing other users, breaking forum guidelines, exploiting the IQ system, or plagiarizing, you will lose your role.</li>
<li>Genius editorial standards are always evolving. Your account will be under review if you've made less than 30 annotations/edits in the last 12+ months. If your work is high quality, then you will remain an editor. If your work needs to be improved, you will be given a warning. You will lose your role if your output continues to be below standard.</li>
</ul>

To stay up to date with best practices and our evolving editorial standards, follow the [Genius Editorial Board Forum](https://genius.com/genius-editorial-board) and [join us on Discord.](https://genius.com/discussions/408236-Welcome-to-the-genius-community-discord-server)

<hr><h2>"Is..." Q&As</h2>

• Is there a live performance of "[Song]" by [Artist]?
○ Yes, there is. [Artist] played "[Song]" in [Location] on [Date]. [Insert YouTube link]
<small>This is to be used for songs that have live performances. [Example](https://genius.com/Mallard-everybody-knows-about-brazzers-lyrics).</small>

• Is there an acoustic performance of "[Song]" by [Artist]?
○ Yes, there is. You can find it here: [Insert performance link].
<small>This is to be used only when an acoustic version isn't on streaming. [Example](https://genius.com/Peach-fuzz-what-do-you-want-from-this-lyrics).</small>

• Is there a BTS video for the song?
○ Yes, there is. On [date of upload, if available], [Person/Label/Company who uploaded it] uploaded a BTS video on [Platform]. [Insert link]

• Is there an alternative title for "[Song]" by [Artist]?
○ Yes, there is. The song was also released as "[Other Name]" on *[Secondary Project/DSP]*.
<small>This is to be used for songs which have different names in different places. [Example](https://genius.com/93feetofsmoke-dracula-freestyle-og-lyrics).</small>

<hr><h2>"Where..." Q&As</h2>

• Where can I listen to "[Song]" by [Artist]?
○ You can listen to "[Song]" on [DSP] by clicking [here](Insert external link here).
<small>Or you can also just insert the external link right away. Keep in mind that this Q&A is to be used for SoundCloud or Bandcamp exclusives. [Example](https://genius.com/Speal-hot-heads-lyrics?question_id=1238662#1238662).</small>

The following formats consist of (optionally) just having the YouTube link as the answer.

• Where can I listen to the full EP/Album?
<small>This is to be used for projects that have a full audio stream on YouTube. [Example](https://genius.com/albums/Footnotes/Everything-last-year?question_id=1234146#).</small>
• Where can I see the BTS video for "[Song]" by [Artist]?
• Where can I see the BTS of the song's music video?
• Where can I see the live perfomance?
• Where can I see the acoustic perfomance?
• Where can I see the Vevo Footnote for the song's music video?

<hr><h2>"What..." Q&As</h2>

• What did [Artist(s)] say about "[Song]"?
<small>This can be answered using any of the following formats:</small>
○ > [What they said]
— [Artist], [where/when they said it]
<small>Or this:</small>
○ From [where/when]:

Artist/Member:
> [What they said]
<small>The latter is for songs by multiple artists (in which each artist added their own words for the song) or bands. [Example](https://genius.com/5-seconds-of-summer-youngblood-lyrics).</small>

• What DAW software was used in the creation of the song?
○ [Artist] used [DAW software] to [mix/master/record] the song.
<small>This is to be used since the "Pro Tools" roles were removed. [Example](https://genius.com/Bon-jovi-color-me-in-lyrics).</small>

• Where and when was the snippet of "[Song]" by [Artist] revealed?
○ [Artist/label/etc.] ["posted the snippet" hyperlinked to the social media post of the snippet] in [social media name], with the following as the caption:

> [The caption copy-pasted from the post]
<small>Note: Other answers are possible too. Answer where and when the snippet was released even if not in social media and hyperlink the sources.</small>

<hr><h2>"Has..." Q&As</h2>

• Has "[Song]" by [Artist] won any awards?
○ <i>Yes. In the [award ceremony] the song won the award for [award title].</i>
○ <i>Yes. On [date, if available], [awarders] awarded "[Song]" for [Award Title].</i>
○ <i>Yes. On [date, if available], [awarders] awarded "[Song]" with [award].</i>
○ <i>It was nominated in [award ceremony or so] for [award], however, it did not win it.</i>
<small>Note: These are to be used for songs with awards or nominations.</small>

• Has this song received any certifications?
○ <i>[Yes,] [Song] was certified [Certification](insert link) by the RIAA on [Date].</i>
○ <i>(Yes,) On [Date], [Song] was certified [Certification](Insert link) by the RIAA.</i>
<small>Note: </small>[<small>Always link the song page on the certification site.</small>](35138131)<small> The website itself does not need to be linked. </small>[<small>The RIAA is the only site for American songs to be  100% trusted.</small>](35138132)

Here are some tips compiled by the Genius community for getting the most accurate lyrics!

<h1>Formatting Checks</h1>

[This tool](https://editor.rdil.rocks/) built by community member [@Reece](https://genius.com/Reece) can flag common formatting and spelling issues.

<h1>Audio Manipulation</h1>

<h3>Modifying Playback Speed</h3>

This can help you hear things and make you have to rewind less.

• YouTube has built-in options to play videos at various speeds
• You can use a browser extension like Video Speed Controller: [Chrome](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/video-speed-controller/nffaoalbilbmmfgbnbgppjihopabppdk) | [Firefox](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/videospeed/)

Be careful though, as sometimes playing something super slow can trick you into hearing things that aren't there. Don't be too confident about what you hear in slow motion.

<h3>Isolating Vocals</h3>

In some cases, you can find existing vocals-only versions. Try searching variations of "[song] vocals/stem/a cappella", etc. Sometimes these are [officially released](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D8pqI_UICfk), while other times, fans have used software.

On that note, you can also make them yourself. Some community members use sites like [Vocal Remover](https://vocalremover.org/) or [lalal.ai](https://lalal.ai/) to isolate a track's vocals.

<h3>Looping</h3>

When transcribing along with a song, you sometimes fall behind and rely on memory, which increases the chance of human error. A way around this issue is looping excerpts with services like [Tune Transcriber](https://www.tunetranscriber.com/). This service allows you to adjust playback speed and loop parts of audio files and YouTube videos.

<h3>Reversing Audio</h3>

Some songs feature [reversed audio](https://genius.com/14913125), which is much easier to transcribe with access to "unreversed" audio.

• Reversed clips are sometimes available on social media, but this is dependent on someone reversing the audio and choosing to upload it.

• Some users reverse the audio themselves with programs like Audacity or with online tools such as this [one](https://www.kapwing.com/tools/reverse), which can reverse the video (and audio) from a YouTube link. There is a 4-minute length limit for exporting videos on the free plan, but you can usually crop the song and just keep the section that you want to reverse. When the "Reverse Video" option is checked, the online editor doesn't always play the audio, but you can create a free account and download the file as an MP3 to get the reversed audio.

<h1>Checklist of Places to Look for High-Quality (Ideally Official) Lyrics</h1>

Be aware that official lyrics are not infallible! But they are definitely a great source. The actual audio always takes precedence.

<h3>Album Liners</h3>

Ideally, you physically own the album! But even if you don't, you can often find album liners via:
• Discogs - [example](https://www.discogs.com/release/7997418-Crucifix-Acid-Reign) (be sure to check several versions of the album if the first one you check doesn't have what you are looking for)
• eBay
• Search engine: try a variation of an image search for "[artist] [song/album] booklet/liner/lyrics" because sometimes they are uploaded to different sites

<h3>Lyrics from Videos</h3>

Sometimes labels release [<i>official</i> lyric videos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NywWB67Z7zQ).

Sometimes official lyrics are in the [description section of their YouTube video(s)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8UVNT4wvIGY). This is also true of some SoundCloud descriptions.

Sometimes, a [song's YouTube video has Closed Captioning with official lyrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qrO4YZeyl0I). Click Show Transcript a little bit above the comment section. The lyrics are more likely to be legit if they have music notes around them, like in the example above, but even these are still often inaccurate.

<h3>Lyrics from Labels</h3>

[Genius sometimes shares official lyrics that it receives from labels](https://genius.com/discussions/333861-Lyrics-from-labels). You can also contact them or artist management for official lyrics.

<h3>Handwritten Lyrics</h3>

Do a search for handwritten lyrics. Just be careful, as not all images of handwritten lyrics are written by the artist.

<h3>Artist Websites</h3>

Some artists post their lyrics on their websites: E.g., [Bob Dylan](https://www.bobdylan.com/songs/).

<h3>Books</h3>

Artists like Jay-Z, Eminem, and others have published books with official lyrics for some of their most popular songs. It's best to own the book, but sometimes you can find what you want via a preview on sites like Amazon or Google Books.

<h3>Ask the Artists</h3>

You can reach out to artists on social media. Artists as popular as Eminem are unlikely to reply, but many appreciate your interest and respond. It doesn't hurt to ask!

<h3>Check Other Performances</h3>

<b>Live Versions</b>

When a line is unclear on the album version, you can sometimes make sense of it when it's delivered differently in a live performance. Of course, be careful since live renditions aren't always perfectly identical to the studio versions.

<b>Cover Versions</b>

These are not definitive since the new performer may not know the actual lyrics. However, if an artist got permission to release an official cover, they may also have access to the official lyrics. At worst, this can shed light on what someone else thinks the lyrics are.

<h3>Other Sites</h3>

[Do <b>NOT</b> copy-paste lyrics!](https://genius.com/30471982) However, checking another site can be a way to get another opinion on what a word is. When looking at existing scribes though, please be aware that [our brains are very good at hearing whatever we read](https://www.ctvnews.ca/sci-tech/what-do-you-hear-why-a-resurfaced-audio-illusion-is-dividing-the-internet-again-1.5045408), whether that is actually what is being said or not.

In addition to all the lyric sites that typically appear in search results (like LyricFind and Musixmatch), consider also checking Apple Music (subscription required). Apple Music gathers lyrics from various sources (including Genius) and sometimes edits them. Lyrics there may even be official, especially if Apple Music had them right at release, suggesting that the artist and/or their record label provided the lyrics themselves. Artists can submit their lyrics directly to Apple Music, either through [certain music distributors](https://genius.com/32835524) or [through Apple Music for Artists](https://artists.apple.com/).

<h3>Annotations</h3>

Sometimes you may think a lyric is wrong, but the annotation on the line may explain why it is actually correct, so it is often wise to check the annotations before changing something. Other times, users will have used annotations to suggest a correction.

<h1>Additional Tip for Correcting Lyrics</h1>

It may be helpful to make the edit alongside a temporary note that says where you got that lyric from. E.g., if you change "old lyric" to "new lyric" based on an album liner, you can make the edit like this:

⤷ New lyric (based on album liner)

Save, and then remove your note and save again. This can help other users understand why you made the change you did.

<h1>Are You Still Stumped?</h1>

<h3>Indicate Uncertainty</h3>

If you are unsure after all these techniques, you can indicate your uncertainty by transcribing the missing part as a question mark between two square brackets, like this: [?]. It can also be helpful to put a timestamp right after, like this: [?][2:04]. If you think you know what the artist says but are uncertain, it can be noted like this: [Uncertain lyric][?]. [It is important to keep the integrity of the [?] to allow for proper searching.](37825298)

Please remember that using [?]s is a sign of a good transcriber. Some artists don't enunciate, don't have their songs mixed properly, or deliver region-specific or personal lyrics. The [?] highlights gaps for other listeners to focus on, which is better than hosting the incorrect lyrics, which may cause people to gloss over them.

<h3>Seek Help</h3>

When there are gaps, you can try reaching out to other skilled transcribers, fans of the artist you need help understanding (e.g., via artist-specific subreddits), or people knowledgeable about the artist's region or country. For example, it may be best to contact an Australian if you need help transcribing a song by an Australian hip-hop artist.

It's often easier to get a hold of another set of ears than the artist, their official lyrics, or their team/label. Genius is a community-driven platform, so use its community! Users regularly help each other out in [this Discord channel](https://discord.com/channels/768245765629018133/768255926003957791).

<h3>Last Resort: Come Back to It!</h3>

When rewatching films, you often notice new details. This principle is also true for music. Returning to an incomplete transcript can make all the difference.

<h1>Build</h1>

Builds, also known as build-ups or rises, are parts in which a song's beat, pitch, or other elements start speeding up, or just generally are increased during a period of a few seconds to a minute in some cases. Builds, as the name implies, are either building up to a beat drop, or a climax.

One example of this can be seen on [Proxy](https://genius.com/Martin-garrix-proxy-lyrics) by [Martin Garrix](https://genius.com/artists/Martin-garrix). The song begins its build-up at approximately 54 seconds in, and proceeds to go up for the next 36 seconds (ending at roughly 1:30).

Build-ups are common in EDM, House, and similar genres, but barely ever seen in other types of music.

<h1>Drop</h1>

Drops, also known as beat drops, are parts in which a song has a sharp change in its beat, typically becoming slower. Drops will often mark the end of a build-up or climax of a song.

One example of this can be seen on ["Up To No Good"](https://genius.com/Manyfew-and-pbh-and-jack-up-to-no-good-lyrics). The song has a drop from 0:24 to 0:54.

Drops are common in EDM, House, and similar genres, but barely ever seen in other types of music.

<h2>Vocal Chops</h2>

Vocal chops are where a vocal is taken and mashed up in a way that uses words or syllables to create rhythm or melody. They should not be transcribed. -> [Click here for examples.](38641733)

Understanding the anatomy of a song is like understanding the blueprint of a house. Once you know what each "room" is designed for, you’ll start hearing your favorite music in a completely new, more vivid way.

Here is a comprehensive, detailed guide on how to identify every major structural component of a song, what its function is, and how to spot it.

---

### 1. The Intro (Introduction)
* **What it is:** The very beginning of the song. 
* **Its Job:** To set the mood, establish the tempo, define the key, and grab your attention. 
* **How to identify it:** The intro is almost always instrumental. It usually lasts 4 to 8 measures. Sometimes, it features a stripped-down version of the chorus melody, a signature guitar riff, or a unique sound effect. Once the main lead vocal begins singing the actual song, the intro is over.

### 2. The Verse
* **What it is:** The "storyteller" of the song.
* **Its Job:** To advance the narrative, build context, and give the listener details. If a song is a movie, the verses are the plot.
* **How to identify it:** 
    * **Lyrics:** The lyrics change from Verse 1 to Verse 2. 
    * **Melody:** Even though the lyrics change, the *melody* (the notes the singer is singing) stays essentially the same in every verse.
    * **Energy:** It usually has a lower dynamic and less intense instrumentation than the chorus. The vocals are often sung in a lower, more conversational register.

### 3. The Pre-Chorus
* **What it is:** The "hype man" or the "runway."
* **Its Job:** To connect the verse to the chorus and build tension, anticipation, and energy so that the chorus hits harder.
* **How to identify it:**
    * It usually appears right after the verse and right before the chorus.
    * You will feel a shift in the music. The drum beat might get faster or drop out entirely, the singer might sing a bit higher or louder, or a new instrument might be introduced. 
    * Unlike verses, the lyrics of a pre-chorus are often exactly the same every time it comes around (though not always).

### 4. The Chorus
* **What it is:** The heart, the climax, and the "hook" of the song.
* **Its Job:** To deliver the main message or theme of the song. It is designed to be the most memorable part of the track—the part you sing in the shower.
* **How to identify it:**
    * **Lyrics & Melody:** Both the lyrics and the melody repeat exactly (or almost exactly) every time the chorus happens. 
    * **Energy:** This is usually the loudest, biggest, and most energetic part of the song. 
    * **Instrumentation:** You will often hear all the instruments playing at once here. Background vocals and harmonies are frequently added to make the sound "wider" and "thicker."
    * **Title:** The title of the song is almost always sung somewhere in the chorus.

### 5. The Post-Chorus
* **What it is:** The "afterparty" of the chorus.
* **Its Job:** To sustain the high energy of the chorus without requiring the listener to process new lyrical information. It gives the listener a chance to dance or groove.
* **How to identify it:** 
    * It happens immediately after the chorus ends.
    * It is highly repetitive and heavily relies on a simple hook. This could be a repetitive vocal chant (like "Oh-oh-oh," "Na-na-na"), or an instrumental "beat drop" (very common in EDM and modern pop).
    * It acts as a cool-down ramp back into the next verse.

### 6. The Refrain
* **What it is:** A repeated line or phrase. (Note: People often confuse "Chorus" and "Refrain," but musically, they are different).
* **Its Job:** To tie a verse together with a central thought.
* **How to identify it:** 
    * Unlike a chorus, a refrain does *not* get its own massive, separate musical section. It is typically nested *inside* the verse, usually as the very first or very last line.
    * **Example:** In Bob Dylan's *"Blowin' in the Wind,"* the verses tell a long story, but every verse ends with the exact same line: *"The answer, my friend, is blowin' in the wind."* That line is a refrain, not a chorus, because it doesn't break away into a new, high-energy musical section.

### 7. The Bridge
* **What it is:** The "plot twist" or the "detour."
* **Its Job:** To provide contrast. By the time a listener has heard two verses and two choruses, their ears might be getting tired of the same chords. The bridge breaks the repetition and brings a fresh sound before the final, biggest chorus.
* **How to identify it:**
    * It usually only happens **once** in a song, typically about 2/3 or 3/4 of the way through (after the second chorus).
    * Everything changes: It often features a brand-new chord progression, a completely different melody, and new lyrics.
    * It changes the emotional tone. If the song is loud, the bridge might suddenly be quiet and intimate. If the song is sad, the bridge might offer a moment of hope or intense anger. 

### 8. The Instrumental Solo / Interlude
* **What it is:** A spotlight for an instrument, or a brief musical breather.
* **Its Job:** To give the singer a break and let the instrumentation carry the emotion of the song.
* **How to identify it:** 
    * **Solo:** A lead instrument (guitar, saxophone, synthesizer) takes over the main melody. It often happens over the chords of the verse, chorus, or bridge.
    * **Interlude:** A short instrumental transition between sections (e.g., a 2-bar drum groove between the first chorus and the second verse).

### 9. The Outro (or Coda)
* **What it is:** The conclusion of the song.
* **Its Job:** To gently (or dramatically) bring the listener out of the song's world and transition them back to silence or into the next track on an album.
* **How to identify it:**
    * It happens at the very end. 
    * It can take several forms: a gradual volume "fade-out," a repeat of the chorus hook that slows down (ritardando), an instrumental jam, or a definitive "hard stop" on a final, resonant chord.

---

### How They All Fit Together (The Standard Pop/Rock Formula)

To truly know these parts, it helps to see how they are usually arranged. The most common commercial song structure in the world looks like this:

1. **Intro** *(Sets the vibe)*
2. **Verse 1** *(Starts the story)*
3. **Pre-Chorus** *(Builds tension)*
4. **Chorus** *(The big catchy hook)*
5. **Verse 2** *(Continues the story)*
6. **Pre-Chorus** *(Builds tension again)*
7. **Chorus** *(The big catchy hook again)*
8. **Bridge** *(The plot twist / emotional peak)*
9. **Chorus** *(Usually the biggest, loudest chorus of the song)*
10. **Outro** *(The song ends)*

### Pro-Tips for Active Listening (How to practice this)
If you want to get good at identifying these parts by ear, listen to a song and ask yourself these questions:
* *"Did the lyrics just change from the last time I heard this melody?"* If yes, you are in a **Verse**.
* *"Are all the instruments suddenly playing louder, and is the singer repeating the song title?"* You are in a **Chorus**.
* *"Did the song just take a completely new direction with chords I haven't heard yet?"* You have hit the **Bridge**.
* *"Is this just a catchy 'la-la-la' part right after the main chorus?"* That’s a **Post-Chorus**.
